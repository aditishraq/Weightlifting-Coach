# Coaching notes for the AI coach

> **DRAFT: needs review by a qualified weightlifting coach.** Written from general coaching practice to ground
> the local AI coach. It is not a coaching standard and has not been checked by a coach. Change anything that
> doesn't match how you coach; the coach reads this file as written.

The app's checks (js/checks.js) report these faults by id. Each section: what it means, typical causes, cues,
drills, and how it connects to other faults. The coach is given only the sections for the faults found on a
lift, plus "General".

## General

- Fix one thing at a time, and start with the earliest fault in the lift: later faults are often caused by
  earlier ones (a bar that drifts forward off the floor tends to swing out at the hips and land forward).
- A fault on one lift is a hint; the same fault over several sessions is a pattern.
- Cues are short, external where possible ("push the floor away", "bar to the hips"), at most two at once.
- Drills are done light (50–70 % of the lift's best) with the same position the cue targets.
- Video measurements from one camera are approximate. Low-confidence findings are worth re-filming before
  changing anything.

## shoulders: shoulders behind the bar off the floor

What it is: at lift-off or as the bar passes the knee, the shoulders are behind the bar instead of over it or
slightly in front.
Typical causes: hips set too low at the start; trying to "squat" the bar up; weak upper back; starting with the
bar too far from the shins.
Cues: "Shoulders over the bar." "Push the knees back as the bar leaves."
Drills: pause pulls with a 2 s pause just below the knee; halting deadlifts to the knee; start-position holds.
Connects to: hips (if the hips are set low they often then shoot up); close1 (bar swings forward to get round
the knees).

## hips: hips rise faster than the shoulders

What it is: between lift-off and the knee the torso tips forward: the hips come up before the bar does.
Typical causes: start position with the hips too low; legs straightening before the back can hold the
position; weak back or tired legs at heavy loads.
Cues: "Chest and hips rise together." "Push the floor away with the whole foot."
Drills: tempo pulls (3 s to the knee); segment deadlifts with pauses at mid-shin and the knee; front squats for
leg strength.
Connects to: shoulders; close2 (a tipped torso tends to swing the hips into the bar later); extension.

## close1: bar drifts forward off the floor

What it is: before the knee the bar moves forward of where it left the floor, instead of straight up or
slightly back toward the shins.
Typical causes: weight on the toes at the start; knees not moving back; bar set too far over the toes;
shoulders behind the bar.
Cues: "Sweep the bar back to the shins." "Weight to mid-foot as it leaves."
Drills: pulls with a 1–2 s pause at mid-shin; deficit pulls (slow); snatch or clean deadlifts focusing on
lats engaged ("bend the bar").
Connects to: shoulders; heels (weight forward); close2 and catchPos (a forward bar early often lands forward).

## heels: heels rise early

What it is: a heel comes off the floor before the bar reaches mid-thigh.
Typical causes: balance on the toes from the start; bar drifting forward; rushing the second pull.
Cues: "Stay flat until the hips." "Pressure through the whole foot."
Drills: pulls from blocks at the knee with a flat-foot pause; slow-tempo clean or snatch deadlifts; balance
drills holding the mid-thigh position.
Connects to: close1 (bar forward pulls weight forward); close2; feet (often lands forward if weight was
forward).
Note: on video, the plate often hides the feet at the start, which makes this check less certain.

## arms: arms bend early

What it is: the elbows bend well before full extension (not the bend at the very end that starts the pull
under).
Typical causes: trying to lift the bar with the arms; anxiety about getting under; not trusting the hip drive.
Cues: "Long arms, ropes." "Legs and hips first, arms last."
Drills: no-arm pulls / high pulls with straight arms; muscle snatch or clean only after extension; tall
snatch or clean to learn the turnover separately.
Connects to: extension (bent arms often cut the extension short); turnover.

## extension: incomplete extension

What it is: at the top of the pull the hips and knees are not open (hip and knee angles well under 180°).
Typical causes: cutting the pull short to get under quickly; bar too far in front (lifter jumps forward
instead of up); early arm pull.
Cues: "Finish tall." "Stand up with the bar, then go under."
Drills: hang pulls from mid-thigh with a pause at full extension; jump shrugs; pulls to full extension without
the pull under.
Connects to: arms; close2; drop to catch (a short pull leaves less height to get under).
Note: at full extension the plate usually hides the hip on side-on video, so the angle is approximate.

## close2: bar swings out at the hips

What it is: between the knee and the top of the pull the bar moves forward of where it left the floor.
Typical causes: bumping the bar forward with the hips instead of brushing it up; bar away from the thighs
before the hips; shoulders behind the bar too early; leaning back at the top.
Cues: "Bar to the hips, then up." "Brush and stand."
Drills: hang snatch or clean from mid-thigh with a light contact; pulls with lats engaged; "slider" pulls
keeping the bar on the thighs.
Connects to: close1; feet (jumping forward to chase the bar); catchPos (bar lands forward).

## feet: feet land out of place

What it is: the feet land clearly forward of where they started (chasing the bar), or far back.
Typical causes: forward: bar out in front (close1, close2). Far back: leaning back at the finish, or swinging
the bar out and jumping back under it. A small hop back and out is common and not a fault in itself.
Cues: "Feet to the same place, wider." "Pull yourself down, not back."
Drills: drop snatch or tall clean with marked foot positions; lifts from the hang with feet landing on tape
marks; no-feet (stay flat) snatches or cleans to remove the jump.
Connects to: close2; catchPos.

## catchPos: bar lands off mid-foot

What it is: in the catch the bar is clearly in front of or behind the middle of the feet.
Typical causes: in front: bar swung forward (close1, close2), or feet jumped back. Behind: leaning back at the
finish.
Cues: "Catch it over the middle of the foot." "Meet the bar, don't chase it."
Drills: snatch or clean balance; pause in the catch for 2–3 s; overhead squats or front squats with pauses.
Connects to: close2; feet.
Note: on video the plate and the lifter are at different depths; this comparison is only judged when the camera
is close to side-on.

## dip: jerk dip not vertical

What it is: during the jerk dip the bar or torso tips forward.
Typical causes: weight onto the toes; dipping with the hips back; elbows dropping.
Cues: "Straight down, straight up." "Knees forward, chest up."
Drills: dip squats with a pause; jerk dips with a 2 s pause at the bottom; push presses.
Connects to: drive.

## drive: jerk drive goes forward

What it is: from the bottom of the dip to lockout the bar travels forward.
Typical causes: a forward dip; pushing the bar forward to clear the face; head not moving through.
Cues: "Drive up and get the head through." "Bar over the back of the neck."
Drills: jerk drives (no split); behind-the-neck jerks; split jerk from the split position.
Connects to: dip; lockout.

## lockout: arms lock out late (press-out)

What it is: the elbows are still bent when the bar arrives overhead and straighten afterwards.
Typical causes: not punching under fast enough; weak lockout position; driving the bar forward.
Cues: "Punch and lock." "Lock it as the feet land."
Drills: jerk balance; behind-the-neck jerk from a split; overhead holds in the split.
Connects to: drive.

## turnover: drop and timing (information)

Not a fault: how far the bar fell between the top of the pull and the catch, the ride down after it, and how
long the turnover took. A long drop can come from a short pull (extension) or a slow turnover. In a squat
catch the bar is received and then rides down with the lifter; the ride down is normal.
