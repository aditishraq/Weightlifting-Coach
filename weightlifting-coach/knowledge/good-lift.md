# What a good lift looks like

A reference for learning without a coach: what each position of the snatch, clean and jerk should look like,
and what published measurements of elite lifters show, with sources. The app compares your lifts with the
measured ranges (Bar path tab, "Compared with elite lifts") and the AI coach reads this file.

**How to read the numbers.** They come from elite lifters at or near their best in competition, filmed side-on.
They describe what elite lifts *look like*, not a target to copy exactly:
- **Load matters:** bar speed and height drop as the weight goes up (Harbili & Alptekin 2014), so a light
  training lift will often be faster and higher than these.
- **Body size matters:** taller lifters pull the bar higher (Gourgoulis et al. 2000).
- **The path type isn't a grade:** elite lifters succeed with all four trajectory types. The study of 319
  elite snatches says weightlifting success can be achieved "with a variety of technique profiles"
  (Cunanan et al. 2020).
- **Ranges** are mean ± SD unless noted. About two thirds of elite lifts fall inside mean ± SD.

The position descriptions below are common coaching descriptions, not measurements; the numbers are from
the studies listed at the end.

## Snatch

### Positions

1. **Start.** Feet about hip width, bar over the middle of the foot. Shoulders over or slightly in front of the
   bar, back flat and tight, arms straight, hips above the knees.
2. **Lift-off to the knee (first pull).** Legs push the floor away; the back angle stays about the same; the
   knees move back out of the bar's way; the bar moves up and slightly **back toward the lifter**.
3. **Knee to the hips (transition).** The knees come forward again under the bar, the torso becomes more
   upright, the bar stays close to the thighs.
4. **Second pull (extension).** Hips and knees extend fast and fully, feet push through the floor, arms stay
   long until the body is extended. The bar moves up and **a little away** from the lifter as the hips meet it.
5. **Turnover.** The lifter pulls themselves under with the arms, the feet move to the receiving stance, the
   bar keeps rising briefly and then falls.
6. **Catch.** The bar is locked overhead in a deep squat, over the middle of the foot, slightly **behind where
   it started**, caught before it falls far.
7. **Recovery.** Stand up with the bar over the middle of the foot.

What separated made from missed snatches at the same weight (61 elite men, Nagao 2019): the missed lifts
dropped further before the catch (Dy3, 18 vs 17 cm); maximum bar height and peak bar speed did not differ.

Trajectory types at the 2015 Worlds (Cunanan 2020): type 3 (away–toward–away–toward) 53 %, type 2 (never crosses
the start line) 28 %, type 1 (crosses it going away) 13 %, type 4 6 %. In Gourgoulis 2000, 9 of 12 lifters'
bar never crossed the start line.

## Clean

### Positions

The same pull as the snatch with a narrower, shoulder-width grip, so the bar is caught lower:

1. **Start**, **first pull**, **transition** and **second pull** as in the snatch: the bar sweeps back toward
   the shins in the first pull, stays close to the thighs, the lifter extends fully before pulling under.
2. **Turnover.** The elbows move around the bar fast so the bar lands on the shoulders (front rack), elbows
   high.
3. **Catch.** The bar is received on the shoulders, then the lifter rides down with it into the front squat;
   the ride down is normal and not part of the "drop".
4. **Recovery.** Stand up with the elbows up and the bar over the middle of the foot.

### Measured in elite cleans

**No open elite reference ranges found.** The searches turned up no openly available study reporting the
clean's bar path (toward/away/catch displacements), drop or catch height for elite lifters, so the app shows
no clean comparison. What is reported: the clean shows less vertical bar travel and lower bar speed than the
snatch (Kinematic comparison of snatch and clean with IMU sensors, PAAH; snatch vs clean in elite lifters,
J Biomech 2025, abstract only). The snatch's horizontal pattern (back toward the lifter in the first pull, a
small loop at the hips, caught behind the start) is the usual coaching model for the clean as well.

## Jerk

### Positions

1. **Rack.** Bar on the shoulders, elbows slightly in front of the bar, feet about hip width.
2. **Dip.** Knees bend straight forward over the toes, torso stays upright, the bar travels straight down;
   short and controlled.
3. **Drive.** Reverse sharply and drive the bar straight up with the legs; the head moves back out of the way.
4. **Split (or squat / power) and catch.** Push under the bar into the split, arms lock as the feet land, bar
   over the back of the neck, roughly over the middle of the base.
5. **Recovery.** Front foot back first, then the back foot, bar steady overhead.

### Measured in elite jerks

| What | Elite range | App measure | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Peak bar speed in the drive | split jerk 1.53 ± 0.08 m/s, squat jerk 1.52 ± 0.14 m/s | Drive speed | Liu 2024 (elite men, 61–81 kg) |
| Dip (active pre-squat) time | split 0.23 ± 0.07 s | – | Liu 2024 |
| Bar drop into the split catch | about 4 cm | – | Liu 2024 |
| Dip depth | 16.8–20.9 cm (9.9–12.3 % of the lifter's height) in three lifts by **one** world champion | Dip depth | Prassas & Fulton (ISBS) |

The dip-depth figure is from a single lifter and is shown as an example, not a range.
